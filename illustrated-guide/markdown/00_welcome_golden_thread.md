# Welcome: the golden thread

## Why this guide exists

This is an illustrated, beginner-friendly companion to the full "MALDI-TOF Study Guide," built around the real Bruker UltrafleXtreme MALDI-TOF/TOF instrument in Aalto University's Molecular Biotechnology lab. It mirrors all 17 topics of that guide, but slows down, adds a figure to every topic, and keeps technical terms paired with a plain-language explanation the first time they appear. It is written for a bachelor's student who has never touched a mass spectrometer before.

Seventeen tabs can feel like a lot of separate facts. They are not. Underneath all of them sit the same five ideas, repeated in a new setting each time. Spotting them as you move through the guide will make every later tab feel like a variation on something you already understand, rather than a new subject.

## The five ideas that run through everything

**1. One measurement, one equation, every answer.** Every single mass this instrument ever reports comes from timing how long an ion takes to fly down a tube, then solving one equation, t = L × sqrt(m / (2zeV)), for mass. Sample prep, calibration, troubleshooting and every case study are all, underneath, about making that one timing measurement as clean and trustworthy as possible.

**2. A number is only as good as its calibration.** The instrument does not know absolute mass on its own: it has to be shown known masses (calibrants) first, and checked against them regularly. Calibration drift, not a dramatic failure, is the single most common reason a result turns out to be wrong. Every tab on errors, troubleshooting, and judging a "good" result comes back to this.

**3. The mode and the matrix are chosen to fit the molecule, not the other way round.** Small peptides reward reflector mode's sharper peaks; large, intact proteins need linear mode to survive the flight at all. Different matrices suit different jobs (routine peptides versus sialylated sugars versus intact proteins). Picking the wrong one does not just give a worse answer, it can give no usable answer.

**4. One technique, many different questions.** The core capability, measuring exact molecular masses very fast, gets reused for strikingly different jobs across this guide: identifying a digested protein from its peptide masses, mapping a sugar chain onto a peptide, reading a whole intact protein end to end without digesting it, and even telling bacterial species apart in a hospital lab. The physics does not change; only the sample, the matrix, the mode, and the question asked of the resulting spectrum change.

**5. Confidence is a number, not a feeling.** A real identification always comes with a quantitative check: a calibration error in ppm, a MASCOT ions score against a significance threshold, a Biotyper Log(Score) band, a Fragmentation Coverage or Intensity Coverage percentage. Learning to read and judge those numbers, not just the peaks, is what separates a trained user from a beginner.

## How to use this guide

Work through the tabs in order the first time: each one briefly leans on ideas from the one before it. After that, the tabs stand on their own, so you can jump straight to whichever topic you need (the "Cheat Sheet" tab, near the end, is built for exactly that kind of quick lookup). Every tab ends with self-check questions and an answer key: use them to test yourself before moving on, not just as a check to read passively.

A glossary of every technical term introduced across the guide follows this tab. You do not need to memorise it before starting; it is there so that the first time a tab uses a term like PCIS window or oxonium ion, you have somewhere to look it up without losing your place.

## What this illustrated version does and does not cover

This version mirrors the structure and the real facts of the full 17-tab study guide, with added figures, worked examples broken into steps, and self-check questions in every tab. It does not replace the full guide's quick-reference tables and extended citation list; those live in the "Cheat Sheet & Further Reading" tab here in full, and in even more depth in the original guide.
